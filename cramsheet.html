Master Timeline — Numbers Overlaid on Dynasties

One line, all chapters. The pattern to notice: every "fixed" number in EAM history has an earlier and a later version — that's the argument, not a side detail.

~150 BC · Western Han
Mawangdui manuscripts
11 meridians, moxibustion only (no needling), no named acupoints, only 2–3 meridians linked to organs.
Han Dynasty
Huang Di Nei Jing · Shen Nong Ben Cao Jing
Nei Jing standardizes 12 meridians. Shen Nong Ben Cao Jing catalogues 365 medicinals (symbolic — days of the year) in three toxicity grades.
Jin Dynasty (~3rd c.)
ABC of Acupuncture (Huangfu Mi)
Acupoint count: 349.
Tang / Sui, ~1,500 yrs ago
Sun Simiao · Qian Jin Yao Fang
First articulates the true point concept — points found by palpating tenderness/nodules, not measured. Root of Japanese Toyohari's live-point method today.
Song Dynasty
Ji Sheng Jing · Zheng Lei Ben Cao → Taiping Shenghui Fang
Acupoints: 359. Formula count escalates from ~2,800 (Zheng Lei Ben Cao) through ~5,000 (Qian Jin Yao Fang lineage) to 17,800+ (Taiping Shenghui Fang).
Ming Dynasty
Reorganization + Ben Cao Gang Mu
Individual organ-named points (liver point, kidney point) folded into the Bladder meridian — creates its unique forked pathway. Li Shizhen's Ben Cao Gang Mu expands the herbal canon to 1,892 medicinals (+1,527 over Shen Nong Ben Cao Jing).
Qing Dynasty
Acupoint count settles — sort of
Point count around 361 — but even within Qing texts, the number still wasn't universal. Wen Bing Tiao Bian (Wu Jutong) formalizes the Four Levels (Wei/Qi/Ying/Xue) alongside the older Six Stages model.
20th century
Standardization era
"Traditional anatomical chart" is invented in China — never existed before this. 1959: China formalizes the national "TCM" curriculum (15 fused subjects). Point totals keep drifting (new points like the tongue point continue to be added even today).

Cross-Chapter Connection Threads

Choi rewards essay answers that show a term or idea crossing chapter boundaries. These are the threads confirmed live in lecture so far — cite the earlier appearance when the topic resurfaces.

Thread 1 — Six Stages / Gradation Terms

First named in Chapter 2 (Week 4) as Xiao Yang / Tai Yang / Xiao Yin / Tai Yin — Choi's own yin-yang gradation vocabulary, planted before it's "supposed" to appear. Resurfaces formally in Chapter 3 as Zhang Zhong Jing's Six Stages framework in the Shang Han Lun. Resurfaces again in Chapter 6 as the older model being partly superseded by Wu Jutong's Four Levels (Wei/Qi/Ying/Xue) in Wen Bing theory.

Thread 2 — "Nothing Was Fixed 2,000 Years Ago"

Runs through Chapter 1 (Shen Nong myth = compressed empirical trial-and-error, not divine knowledge), Chapter 3 (meridian count 11→12, acupoint count 365→349→359→361, Ming-era reorganization of the Bladder meridian), and Chapter 4 ("TCM" itself is a 1959 standardization, not an ancient monolith). Same argument, four different data sets.

Thread 3 — Analogical Inference (Bi Lei Qu Xiang)

Named explicitly in Chapter 2 as AHM's core epistemological method. Applied concretely in Chapter 1's Week 5 material to herbal medicine's origin story (sour taste → collects energy, by observed/verified effect) and to the worldwide doctrine-of-signatures pattern (kidney beans, walnuts). Same reasoning method, different chapters.

Thread 4 — True Point / Live Point

Concept originates with Sun Simiao in the Chapter 3 Tang-Dynasty material, survives into modern Japanese Toyohari practice (Oshide/Sashite technique) — directly undercuts the "traditional anatomical chart" idea attacked in the same chapter's Week 5 material.
