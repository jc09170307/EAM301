Week 2 — Chronological Overview of the History of Traditional Medicine

1.Which of the following is not a historical characteristic of the Spring and Autumn and Warring States periods?
A) Political turmoil due to the decline of the Zhou Dynasty
B) Diverse philosophical flowering known as the Hundred Schools of Thought (Zhuzi Baijia)
C) Development of military technology and state philosophy
D) Adoption of Buddhism as the state religion and the decline of medicine ✓
Answer & why: Buddhism didn't reach China until the Han Dynasty at the earliest (later spreading via the Silk Road) — it plays no role in the Spring & Autumn/Warring States periods.
✓ verified
2.What medical transition is signified by the change in the radical of the character for medicine (Yi, 醫) from "Shaman" (Wu, 巫) to "Alcohol/Wine" (You, 酉)?
A) A shift from shamanistic healing to rational, pharmacological treatment ✓
B) A shift in focus from acupuncture to surgery
C) Elevation from folk medicine to imperial medicine
D) The introduction of Western medicine and the disappearance of traditional medicine
Answer & why: The character's radical change from 巫 (shaman) to 酉 (wine — used to prepare medicinal decoctions) tracks medicine moving away from spirit-based healing toward herbal/pharmacological practice.
✓ verified
3.What new theoretical system emerged during the Qing Dynasty for the treatment of infectious diseases?
A) 'Shanghan Lun' (Treatise on Cold Damage)
B) 'Wenbing' theory (Warm Disease Theory) ✓
C) 'Pi Wei Lun' (Treatise on the Spleen and Stomach)
D) 'Zi Yin' theory (Nourishing Yin Theory)
Answer & why: Wen Bing (Warm Disease) theory, developed by Ye Tian Shi and later systematized by Wu Jutong, is the Qing-era framework for epidemic febrile disease — already covered in your Chapter 6 material.
✓ verified
4.Which book, written by Wang Qingren during the Qing Dynasty, attempted to correct the errors of past medicine through anatomical observation?
